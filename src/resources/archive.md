*There's a lot of Buddhist books given away freely online, so called Dhammadāna. But where to find them, and which one to read? Here's a selection for you with more than 300 books in total, gathered from different online sources.*

The books are either from modern meditation teachers and scholars, or translations of the Buddhist canon and its commentaries. The main language in the archive is English, but there's also a Swedish and German folder. Later on, more languages might also be added with the help of others.

> *"Dhammadāna is the noblest among practices of gifts, as it lies in offering the knowledge of the dhamma to sentient beings. Gift (dāna) of the teaching on reality (dhamma)."*  
> – Pali English Glossary

## A quick walkthrough of the archive

All the books by modern writers are put in the folder Various Authors. In Buddhist Canon & Tipitaka you find translations of old scriptures that all Buddhists consider sacred and important, plus some commentaries. In the folder Chanting Books you find different compilations of holy texts meant to be used for chanting aloud and to do ceremonial practice. In Dictionaries, Guides etc. you find helpful books about the Pali language or books that tries to explain a certain topic. In Swedish Dhamma Writings you find books and translations in the Swedish language. Same with German Dhamma Writings.

The books are shown in alphabetical order by title. Use the folder buttons above the shelf to see one folder at a time.

## New to e-books?

The books included in the Dhamma-Dāna Archive are either in the standard formats PDF or EPUB. The PDF's you can read on your computer or tablet, or print easily. The format EPUB are made especially with the intention to be read on an e-book reader, but can also be read on any other device.

If you’re looking for an easy way to read books offline on your Android smartphone or tablet, you might want to download the free app [ReadEra](https://readera.org/). With this app you can read almost any book or document, and easily manage and sort your books. Here’s also [ten more apps](https://www.pcmag.com/how-to/free-ebook-reader-apps-for-smartphone-or-tablet) you can download and use, covering all formats.

If you want to have the best possible experience reading these digital books, then get a proper e-book reader. Read about e-book readers [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-reader). To arrange a digital library on your computer, you can use a free application called [Calibre](https://calibre-ebook.com/). With this you can sort and get an overview of your books, and then easily send or convert them to the file-format you need for your e-book reader or Kindle. Calibre also has a built-in e-book viewer so you can read the books directly on the computer, and works with Mac OS, Windows and Linux.
