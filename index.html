About

There is a crack in everything,
that’s how the light gets in.

– Leonard Cohen

The way in is the way out…

The Internet can feel like a maze, built to hold our attention and make us forget where we were going. Nibbidā is meant to do the opposite. The word means disenchantment, a quiet turning away from what does not lead to freedom. Here nothing is sold, and nothing is asking for your attention.

…and the way out is through.

Nibbidā is a small, independent Dhamma publisher. It prepares books and resources for people who want to understand their own minds more deeply, and who, like the Buddha, take it that there is a way out of the labyrinth for those who walk through it with growing clarity and wisdom. Meditation is the core of that work. Around it there is much that helps: the texts, the languages, the places to practice.

I hope the work here is useful, and that some of you will want to take part in it. May it make your own study and practice a little easier.

Jayadhamma Bhikkhu

A passage to sit with

Insight is the goal. Calm is the means. As long as there is no calm in the mind, there are waves, waves of like and dislike. The waves obscure our vision.

Ayya Khema · Chapter beginning quote (BNGN p. 33)

Quoted with permission.

Only when we experience something entirely new do we understand that the mind has the capacity to think differently.

Ayya Khema · The capacity to think differently (BAI p. 35)

Quoted with permission.

There is no need for fear; as long as there is life, there is breath. As the breath becomes shallow and quiet, a pleasant physical feeling arises.

Ayya Khema · A useful key (WOOH p. 106)

Quoted with permission.

In order to understand the Buddha’s teaching, we have to get below the surface, otherwise our insights will be superficial. The calm mind is the means for delving below relative reality. While we are trying to become calm, at the same time we are objectively examining all that arises, so that there is more and more support for letting go of the thinking.
