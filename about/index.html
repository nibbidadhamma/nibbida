About

In the year 2012, I experienced my first meditation retreat. It blew my mind. Finally, I had found a technique and tool to explore the workings of my inner world. From then on, I always had the option to sit down and face my situation completely, usually by focusing on the breath. “Simple, but not easy,” as the saying goes!

My initial enthusiasm for meditation and its benefits hasn’t left me over the years. The seed of Dhamma and the Buddhist teachings has now grown into a small flower within me, that I water each day by continuing to meditate and try being a good human being.

To deepen my practice and understanding of the Dhamma even further, I made the rather big decision in 2020 to take up the life of a Buddhist monk in the Forest tradition, which emphasizes meditation and simplicity. I'm still keeping the robes, which seem to get a little more comfortable each year!

The wish to spread information about how and where to meditate was the main reason I began building on this website back in the day. Since then, it has grown into something more dynamic and expansive. In fact, it has even become a way for me to deepen my own understanding of Buddhism, meditation, and other related skills for successful inner travel.

I hope you enjoy this initiative! May the work here on the site support your own exploration of meditation and Dhamma, perhaps even make it more fruitful.

With mettā,
/Jayadhamma Bhikkhu (Hampus Haraldssson)

The informative part

In recent years, I’ve been living as a fully ordained Theravāda monk, called a bhikkhu. This journey began in a forest monastery in Myanmar within the Pa-Auk tradition, during the strange times of Covid. At the moment, I’m back in the West, exploring different communities and approaches to life as a forest monk.

Most of the website is in Swedish, my native tongue. The Dhamma is still small in Sweden, which has given me an extra push to make an effort online. There’s a lot to do—things to write about, translate, and find the right words for, in order to shape an adequate Swedish language for describing what goes on in the mind.

Many of the images on this site are taken from different free web resources such as Freepik, Rawpixel, Wikimedia, and others. In a few cases, I’ve also allowed myself to use images where I couldn’t find a copyright holder, even after searching with Google Lens. If you still see something here you don’t like, feel free to write to me and I’ll take it down.
